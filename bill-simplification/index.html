Bill Simplification

Turning a bill that only listed charges into one that explains them, before customers have to call and ask.

The challenge

People weren't calling because billing was complex. They were calling because the bill didn't explain itself.

28Munique billing customers across Postpaid, Wi-Fi and Airtel Black
4.5 lakhbilling interactions with support every month
21%of all assisted interactions, the second-largest contact reason

The support path had three layers, and the first one failed most often. The app showed charges without saying why they existed. Chat had defined journeys but only resolved some queries. Everything else ended in a call, handled manually.

Inside Postpaid alone, the reasons were concentrated. Late fees drove six in ten contacts. That made one charge type the clearest place to start.

The existing experience

A reactive bill. It explained things only after customers were already confused.

  • Callouts were fear-inducing: "services will be stopped" led the message.
  • Customers found unexpected charges only after the bill was generated.
  • The bill showed charges but not the reason behind them.
  • Explanations existed only for people who went looking.
  • Recurring, one-time and prorated charges looked the same.
  • System terms like "Adjustments" and "Other usage charges" meant nothing to customers.

Research

15+ apps that people already trust with money showed the same three patterns.

We benchmarked ride-hailing, commerce, food delivery, rentals and logistics apps for how they present a total, disclose changes and use colour.

Content principles

Explain before they ask

If we already know why an amount changed, say so upfront instead of making customers search for it.

Lead with what matters

Start with what happened and how much it changes the bill. Reveal detail progressively.

Use customers' words

Replace internal billing terms with labels people recognise, and attach context to every charge.

Wireframes & explorations

Five ways to answer "why is my bill this amount?"

Before settling the bill details screen, we tested where the total, the pay action and the explanation for a change should sit, and how loudly a late-fee waiver should speak.

  • Every option leads with the total and a one-line reason for the change, such as "Bill is ₹123 higher than last bill".
  • Trend graphs (A, E) added context but pushed the breakup below the fold.
  • The waiver callout (C) became the pattern carried into the overdue design: the struck-out fee next to ₹0.00.

First approach · Due & overdue

Communicate urgency without creating anxiety.

Late fees were the single largest contact reason, so we started with the due and overdue states. Each message moves from what happened, to why, to what the customer can do next.

What happened→Why it happened→What you can do next
  • Shortened the NBA card to one amount, one date and one consequence.
  • Showed the post-due amount in the bill header, so the fee was visible before it applied.
  • Replaced warning-led copy with informative messages.
  • Surfaced waiver eligibility upfront for eligible customers.
  • Labelled the waiver directly on the bill amount.
  • Grouped charges so the breakup could be scanned.

Design win · Changing the construct

Some confusion can't be fixed with copy. The late fee was billed in the wrong month.

Original construct

Late fee appears on the next month's bill

A customer who paid late in June saw the fee arrive in July's bill, disconnected from the delay that caused it. No label could make that timing feel fair.

Proposed construct

Charge it when it becomes due

Apply the late fee at the moment the bill goes overdue, then show it as already settled on the next invoice. Clearer UI explanations support it at both points.

Second approach · Estimated bills

From explaining a problem to preparing people for it.

The first approach helped customers after the bill arrived. Then we asked: what if we explained the impact before the bill was generated? Instead of waiting for "Why is my bill so high?", the app answers it in advance.

  • Introduced estimated bills, starting with a customer's first partial cycle.
  • Showed how many days each charge applied to, e.g. 19–30 May (12 days).
  • Gave context on upcoming charges and what could still change.
  • Moved the experience to the category landing page, next to related actions like AutoPay.

Reflection

Billing clarity is an end-to-end product capability.

The bill screen was only one moment. Clarity had to hold across the nudge that announces a change, the card that leads to the bill, the hierarchy that reconciles the amount, and the actions that resolve a concern in place.

The biggest lesson was knowing when to stop writing. When the late fee was billed in the wrong month, better copy would only have explained a bad rule. Changing the rule made the explanation unnecessary.
